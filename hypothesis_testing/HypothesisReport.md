We ran a Welch two-sample t-test to see whether the average number of daily bike rentals differs between clear weather days and misty/cloudy days.

The null hypothesis was that the two population means were equal, and the alternative hypothesis was that they are not. We used a significance level of 0.05.

The test gave a t-statistic of 5.8196, approximately 519.19 degrees of freedom and a p-value of 1.033 x 10^-8. Since the p-value is less than 0.05, we reject the null hypothesis.

clear days had an average of 4876.79 rentals compared to the 4035.87 rentals on misty/cloudy days. a difference of about 841 rentals per day. The 95% confidence interval for the difference was 557.05 to 1124.80 rentals, suggesting that the population mean difference falls within this range

There is strong evidence of a difference in average daily bike rentals between clear and misty/cloudy days based on our t-tests. however, we cannot say that weather directly caused this difference because the other factors such as seasons and temperature could also affect bike rentals. rentals on consecutive days may also be related which could violate the independence assumption of the t-test. So while we found a difference in bike rentals, we can't say for sure that weather was the only reason behind it
